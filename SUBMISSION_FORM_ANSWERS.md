### Field 7: A short note on how you got there and which tools you used (Copy-Paste)
```text
To ground recommendations in authentic commuter feedback, I avoided assumptions and directly reverse-engineered Google Play Store's web batch RPC protocol (identifying the active 'oCPfdb' endpoint for package com.moveinsync.ets). Using a custom PowerShell/.NET client, I extracted and normalized a multi-batch dataset of 306 verified public reviews across recent and historical releases.

I then built a semantic classification pipeline mapping feedback against the 7 stages of the employee commute journey (Onboarding, Self-Booking, Routing/ETA, Live GPS Tracking, Boarding/OTP, Fleet Operations, and App Framework Stability). This empirical analysis revealed that recent run-rate ratings dropped to 3.77/5.0 with 30% negative sentiment, driven by: (1) Misdirected operational fleet delays vented onto the Play Store due to unassisted in-app escalation (50.5%); (2) High-friction UX dark patterns, notably an unskippable post-trip ride rating modal and an intrusive 'Seatbelt Safety' popup intercepting OTP clicks during boarding; (3) Map auto-zoom resetting manual zooms every 3-4s; and (4) SQLite Room DB migration crashes post-update. Additionally, MoveInSync had a 0% developer reply rate on negative reviews.

To address this, I formulated a 3-horizon engineering roadmap (P0 Immediate Week-1 Quick Wins, P1 Core Product Hardening, P2 Operational Interception) complete with Jira-ready specifications. Finally, recognizing that 'How are our Play Store ratings?' is a recurring leadership question, I built a production-ready CLI Sentinel tool (moveinsync_rating_sentinel.ps1) for continuous CI/CD monitoring, alongside a zero-dependency interactive executive dashboard (MoveInSync_Rating_Intelligence_Dashboard.html) featuring live KPI telemetry, Chart.js analytics, and a searchable commuter review explorer.
```
